## Do Not Say

- Do not say her ballot was one of the 1,600 ruled fraudulent. Those papers were on a campaigner’s schedule. The handwriting sample of 201 did not include her. The judgment does not name her.
- Do not say she was an MP in 2004. She was 23 and training as a barrister. She entered Parliament in 2010.
- Do not say the whole Birmingham election was thrown out in June 2004. Mawrey voided two wards, Aston and Bordesley Green, on 4 April 2005.
- Do not say her father handed out the rigged ballots. He was the Labour agent in Bordesley Green and witnessed family forms. Petitioners accused him. The judgment does not name him.
- Do not say the case was buried. It led the national news, the councillors were banned, and the wards voted again that July. One Aston councillor, Muhammed Afzal, later won an appeal.
- Do not compare this case to a child sexual exploitation cover-up. Nothing in the judgment or the reporting supports that.
